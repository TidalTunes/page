# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Academics, prospective employers, collaborators, and a general professional audience arriving from places such as LinkedIn. Visitors may have different levels of subject knowledge, but the portfolio may assume technical literacy and should not dilute project material solely for broad accessibility.

## Product Purpose

Present Kashi Tuteja's mathematics and computer science projects as a quiet digital exhibition. Success means a visitor can experience the visual character of the work first, understand each project through a concise technical caption, and reach its code or supporting materials without friction.

## Brand Personality

Precise, contemplative, technical. The experience should feel like a quiet contemporary museum: spatial, composed, and confident enough to leave room around the work.

## Anti-references

Avoid startup-style hero sections, dashboard interfaces, uniform project-card grids, heavy decoration, generic developer portfolios, and unnecessary explanatory text. Avoid reshaping the work to appeal to a lay audience when that would weaken its technical character.

## Design Principles

1. Let the work occupy the room. Visuals carry the first impression; interface chrome recedes.
2. Treat each project as an installation. Vary scale, placement, and medium while maintaining a coherent exhibition rhythm.
3. Write only what earns its space. Use compact technical captions and direct material links.
4. Make complexity feel calm. Mathematical depth can remain intact without visual clutter.
5. Preserve the exhibition without motion. Every animated or interactive work has a purposeful static counterpart.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA contrast and semantic keyboard-accessible navigation. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; replace simulations, video, and active movement with representative still images rather than merely pausing them in an ambiguous state. Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
